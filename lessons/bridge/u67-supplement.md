### Passage

**A** In the fictional harbour town of Eska, a metal weather vane is removed from a warehouse roof during repairs. It later appears in a museum case labelled as a navigation instrument. The description sounds plausible because the object once turned with the wind near a busy port. A researcher asks a narrower question about its actual use. The vane indicated wind direction from its rooftop position, but the surviving records do not show sailors using it to plan voyages. A relationship to the harbour does not establish every maritime function. The researcher begins by distinguishing the object's observed action, its documented setting and the stronger story attached to it after removal.

**B** An early maintenance bill names the warehouse owner and records payment for a new bearing. The bearing allowed the vane to turn around its vertical support. A later photograph shows the vane beside a fixed pointer, which helped viewers relate its movement to a direction. Neither source records wind speed. The researcher notes that a rotating object can invite more claims than its design supports if the observer does not ask which quantity is represented. The bill supplies evidence of maintenance, while the photograph supplies evidence of visible arrangement. Together they clarify part of the object's working life without establishing everything that people may have inferred from it.

**C** During roof repairs, workers attach a numbered tag to the removed vane and place it in a temporary store. A transfer sheet later records delivery to the museum. The number on the tag matches the sheet, providing a link between the rooftop object and the accession entry. This connection matters because another similar vane appears in a dealer's photograph from the same decade. Similar shape alone would not securely identify the museum object. The documented transfer gives the researcher a stronger basis for its provenance, while the dealer's image remains a separate source whose relationship to the object has not been established.

**D** The museum cleans the surface and mounts the vane so that it can no longer rotate. This improves stability inside the case but changes the action a visitor can observe. A small video shows a replica turning outdoors, and the display identifies it as a demonstration made recently. The film helps explain the mechanism without pretending to record the original warehouse in operation. The researcher also asks the museum to keep a treatment log describing cleaning and mounting. Future viewers may otherwise mistake the current appearance for the object's condition at the moment it left the roof, overlooking decisions made during its transition into a collection.

**E** A donor suggests restoring the missing painted emblem visible in an old picture. The photograph confirms an outline but provides too little detail to recover the original colours. The museum therefore places a line drawing beside the object and leaves the metal unchanged. The drawing is labelled as a reconstruction of the visible outline. This choice preserves a distinction between supported form and uncertain colour. It does not establish that the emblem was originally colourless. The display gives visitors enough information to understand why the absent paint has become a historical question rather than silently converting uncertainty into a confident decorative addition.

**F** The revised label describes the vane's rooftop function, recorded transfer and museum treatment. It also explains the limits of the navigation claim. The object now supports several connected investigations into weather observation, building maintenance and collecting. Its later history has not erased its earlier one, but neither can the later presentation be treated as an untouched continuation of use on the roof. A careful account follows the changes in setting and function while preserving the evidence for each stage. Visitors can then appreciate the object without having to accept a more dramatic story than the records support. The museum's explanation becomes stronger when it shows how its claims were assembled and where another document could still change the account.
