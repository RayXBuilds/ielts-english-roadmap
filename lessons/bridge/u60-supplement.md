## SR-01 · Counting Moths beside a Lamp

### Passage

**A** In the fictional field station of Aven, volunteers count insects arriving at a lamp after sunset. Their project begins with a practical question about how observations change when the lamp moves. It does not begin with a census of every insect in the surrounding valley. On the first evening, the lamp stands beside a hedge. On the second, it stands in an open courtyard. The volunteers record forty arrivals at the hedge and twenty at the courtyard. Those figures describe visits to a particular observation point. They do not establish that the valley's insect population fell by half overnight. The team keeps this distinction visible because a simple number can seem to describe a much larger area than the method actually covered.

**B** Each observation session lasts thirty minutes. Volunteers use the same lamp and begin at the same interval after sunset, rather than at the same clock time. They record wind and rain but do not control either. The two sites are observed on different evenings because only one lamp is available. This arrangement leaves the effect of location mixed with possible differences between nights. The project coordinator therefore labels the comparison preliminary. She proposes alternating the sites over several evenings before deciding whether the first contrast persists. Alternation would provide additional observations, although it would not magically make every evening identical or guarantee that all relevant influences had been measured.

**C** Counting requires a rule about repeated visits. A moth may land, leave and return. The volunteers cannot reliably recognise every individual, so they count arrivals rather than unique animals. A mark is added whenever an insect crosses a painted boundary around the illuminated sheet. An insect remaining within that boundary is not counted again simply because it changes position. The rule makes different observers' records more comparable. It also limits the interpretation of the total. Forty arrivals could involve fewer than forty animals, and the record does not allow the team to determine exactly how many individuals returned during that session.

**D** The volunteers photograph uncertain examples for later identification. Some images are too blurred to classify, and these remain in an unresolved category. The coordinator refuses to assign them to the most common group merely to produce a complete-looking table. Two experienced readers examine the clear images independently before discussing disagreements. Their agreement improves confidence in those classifications but does not identify the animals in the blurred images. The original image files are retained alongside revised labels so that a later reviewer can distinguish what was photographed from what someone concluded about it. This separation preserves a path for correction when the available knowledge changes.

**E** A school group asks whether a more powerful lamp would produce a better survey. The coordinator explains that a change in equipment would create a new comparison. A larger count could reflect a different attraction distance or a different response to the light. Within this fictional project, neither explanation has yet been tested. The team records the lamp model and its position for every session so that equipment changes do not disappear inside one continuous column of numbers. A more conspicuous observation is not automatically a more representative one. The appropriate equipment depends on the question and on how its effects on the observations will be investigated.

**F** At the end of the week, the team publishes the arrival totals with dates, locations, session lengths and unresolved classifications. It describes the results as observations around the lamp and invites further work on how those observations relate to the wider valley. No payment information about volunteers appears in the report. Readers can inspect what was counted, but several broader questions remain open. The value of the work lies in making those questions precise enough for another investigation. A modest claim supported by a reproducible procedure can provide a firmer starting point than a dramatic population estimate assembled from numbers that measured a different event.

4. The observers controlled the wind.

6. Arrival totals can include repeat visitors.

### 语境词汇

- **census** 对全部成员的普查
- **preliminary** 仍需更多观察的初步结果
- **alternating** 在不同站点间交替
- **boundary** 决定计数的空间边界
- **unresolved** 暂不能确定分类的
- **reproducible** 程序可被再次按相同规则执行的
