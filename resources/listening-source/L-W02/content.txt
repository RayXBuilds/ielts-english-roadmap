The museum was originally a ____.|warehouse|Welcome to the harbour museum. Before you explore, I'll explain a few features of the building and the activities available today. This building was originally a warehouse, used for goods arriving by boat. It wasn't a passenger station, although people sometimes assume that because of the large entrance hall. The wide doors and heavy floor were designed for moving and storing cargo. During the conversion, the architects retained these features so that the building itself could help tell the story of the harbour.
The oldest exhibits are on the ____ floor.|ground|The oldest exhibits are on the ground floor. They show how small boats connected settlements before the harbour became a major trading place. You can follow the displays in date order, but you don't have to. Each section has a short introduction that makes sense on its own. If you're visiting with somebody who prefers a different pace, agree where to meet rather than feeling you must read every label together. The seating area near the entrance is a convenient meeting point.
Visitors may handle objects on the ____ table.|discovery|Most objects are behind glass, but those on the discovery table may be handled. A volunteer will explain how to lift and examine them safely. These particular objects were selected because touching them helps visitors understand their weight or texture without damaging a fragile surface. Please don't assume the same permission applies elsewhere. A small wooden object in another room may look robust but have cracks that aren't visible. If you're uncertain, ask a member of staff before reaching for anything.
The guided tour begins at ____ a.m.|11.30|Our guided tour begins at eleven thirty this morning. You don't need to book, but please gather near the large clock a few minutes beforehand. The tour lasts about half an hour and concentrates on a few objects rather than covering every room. This means you'll still have time to explore independently afterwards. If you miss the beginning, you're welcome to join quietly. However, the guide may not be able to repeat the introduction while moving the rest of the group through a narrow gallery.
The temporary exhibition concerns harbour ____.|workers|The temporary exhibition upstairs concerns harbour workers. Instead of concentrating on famous ships, it looks at the people who loaded goods, maintained equipment and organised movements along the waterfront. Some displays include personal accounts, while others use workplace records. These sources do different things: a record may tell us how many hours were worked, whereas a personal account can describe how the work felt. Neither gives a complete picture alone. The exhibition invites visitors to compare them and notice both agreements and gaps.

The main comparison will be between different ____ of day.|times|Student two: I wanted to compare first-year students with final-year students, but we couldn't identify them reliably just by watching. ~ Tutor: Exactly. Each observation session will last ____ minutes.|20|Student one: Would an hour at each time be enough? The recording sheet should include the ____.|weather|Student two: Our sheet has spaces for the time, number of people and the activities we observe. The lecture focuses on product ____.|repair|This lecture considers product repair as a process involving design, information and organisation. It's tempting to think that repairing an object is simply a matter of finding someone with the right practical skills. Those skills matter, but the task can become impossible if a component cannot be reached or a replacement cannot be identified. We'll examine a hypothetical household appliance to see how several small decisions made before sale can influence what happens when something eventually stops working.
The first obstacle is access to internal ____.|components|The first obstacle is access to internal components. A case held together with ordinary screws can usually be opened without destroying it. A case permanently bonded along every edge presents a different problem. The technician may be able to identify the fault but still be unable to reach it safely. Ease of opening is therefore not merely a convenience. It can determine whether repair is a realistic option at all. Design evaluation needs to consider the whole sequence of opening, working and reassembling.
Standard fasteners reduce the need for specialist ____.|tools|Standard fasteners can reduce the need for specialist tools. This doesn't mean that every repair should be attempted by an inexperienced owner. Some equipment requires training and appropriate precautions. The point is that unnecessary variation can make even a routine professional task slower or more expensive. If a service centre needs a different tool for every model, maintaining its equipment becomes harder. A design that uses familiar fasteners may support repair while still making clear which procedures require qualified assistance.
A clear diagram helps identify the correct ____.|part|Once the case is open, a clear diagram helps identify the correct part. Similar-looking components may perform different functions or belong to different versions of the same product. Ordering by appearance alone can lead to errors and further delays. Useful documentation links a component's position to a reliable identifier. It should also explain whether a replacement is compatible with older models. Information that makes sense only to the original designer is not necessarily sufficient for somebody maintaining the product several years later.
Replacement supplies depend on a reliable ____.|inventory|Replacement supplies depend on a reliable inventory. A manufacturer may advertise that parts are available, yet a repair still fails if the required item cannot be located or ordered. Inventory records connect physical stock with the information seen by service staff. Inaccurate records can produce repeated unsuccessful orders and damage confidence in repair. Keeping every possible part forever may be impractical, but the period of support should be clear. Customers need realistic expectations rather than a broad promise with no stated conditions.
Repair cost includes the technician's ____.|labour|Repair cost includes the technician's labour as well as the price of the component. A very inexpensive switch may be costly to replace if reaching it requires dismantling most of the appliance. This connects repair economics directly to design. Reducing the number of unnecessary steps can matter more than reducing the price of a small spare part. Any comparison with buying a replacement product should therefore use the full repair cost, while recognising that prices and available services differ between locations.
Owners may reject repair because of ____.|inconvenience|Owners may reject repair because of inconvenience, even when the quoted price seems reasonable. They may need the appliance every day, lack transport to a service centre, or be uncertain about how long the work will take. A repair service that gives clear appointments and realistic completion estimates addresses a different barrier from one that merely lowers prices. Researchers studying decisions should ask about these practical circumstances instead of assuming that every refusal shows indifference to waste or lack of understanding.
A shared workshop can support informal ____.|learning|A shared workshop can support informal learning. Participants see how an experienced person approaches a fault, asks questions and tests possible explanations. This differs from watching a short video that moves directly from problem to solution. In a workshop, uncertainty and unsuccessful attempts are visible too. However, such events require boundaries: volunteers should not be expected to work on equipment beyond their competence. Successful learning includes recognising when to stop and seek qualified help, rather than treating persistence as the answer to every problem.
Evaluation should track how long repairs ____.|last|Evaluation should track how long repairs last. Counting the number of objects repaired during an event provides a useful immediate measure, but it doesn't establish whether those objects remain in use. Follow-up can reveal recurring faults or replacements that fail quickly. It can also be difficult, because participants may not respond later. Researchers should report that loss of information instead of assuming every unreported repair remains successful. A modest conclusion supported by follow-up is stronger than an impressive total whose meaning has never been checked.
The proposed policy starts with a limited ____.|trial|The proposed policy therefore starts with a limited trial involving a defined group of products and service providers. The trial should record costs, waiting times and outcomes, including unsuccessful attempts. It can help reveal which barriers are most significant locally and whether changing one creates pressure elsewhere. For example, greater demand may be encouraging but exceed the capacity of available technicians. The purpose is to learn how the system operates before expanding it, with explicit criteria for deciding what should be retained or changed.
